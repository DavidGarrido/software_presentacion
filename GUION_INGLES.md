## SLIDE 2 — WHERE IT IS (40 seconds)

"Villa de Leyva is located in the **Boyacá department**, in the center of Colombia. It is approximately **157 kilometers from Bogotá** — about three and a half hours by bus — and only 40 kilometers from Tunja, the capital of Boyacá.

The town sits at **2,149 meters above sea level**, which gives it a cool and pleasant climate all year round.

Villa de Leyva was **founded on June 12th, 1572**, and in 1954 it was declared a **National Monument** by the Colombian government, which means its colonial architecture has been carefully preserved for more than four hundred years."

---

## SLIDE 3 — WHAT IT IS LIKE (40 seconds)

"So, what is Villa de Leyva like?

It is a **historic, peaceful and beautiful** town. Compared to other cities, Villa de Leyva is **smaller than Bogotá** but **older than Cartagena**. Its streets are **whiter and wider** than in most Colombian towns.

The weather is **cooler and drier** than the coast — perfect for walking and sightseeing. And the atmosphere is **more charming and relaxing** than a big city.

In one word, Villa de Leyva feels like **stepping back in time**."

---

## SLIDE 4 — ELEMENTS (45 seconds)

"Villa de Leyva has many amazing elements.

First, there is the famous **Plaza Mayor** — one of the largest cobblestone squares in all of South America. There are also beautiful **colonial churches**, like the Parish Church and the Santo Ecce Homo Convent.

For nature lovers, there is **Pozo Azul** — a stunning natural turquoise pool — and a beautiful **waterfall** called La Periquera.

History enthusiasts can visit **El Fósil museum**, where a real dinosaur fossil — a Kronosaurus from 110 million years ago — is on display. And just outside the town is **El Infiernito**, a pre-Columbian stone observatory built by the Muisca people.

Finally, the town is full of **white colonial houses**, craft shops and cozy restaurants."

---

## SLIDE 5 — PLAZA MAYOR (50 seconds)

"The most important and iconic element of Villa de Leyva is, without a doubt, the **Plaza Mayor**.

This square is enormous — it covers **14,000 square meters** — and it is entirely paved with **large cobblestones**. It is surrounded by beautiful **white colonial buildings**, and at its center stands a **stone fountain built in 1617**.

Facing the square is the **Parish Church**, which dates back to 1608.

The Plaza Mayor is popular for many reasons. It is a **symbol of Colombian heritage**. Every year it hosts the famous **Star and Kite Festival**. Tourists come to enjoy the sunset, explore the craft shops, and eat at the restaurants around it. It has even been used as a **filming location** for movies and TV series.

The Plaza Mayor is not just a square — it is a **living museum** where time seems to stop."
